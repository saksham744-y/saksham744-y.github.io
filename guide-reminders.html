Using reminders to keep a daily routine

A reminder helps when it arrives at the right moment with enough warning to change what you are doing. Too many reminders, or ones that arrive too late, get ignored.

1. Remind only the tasks that matter

If every item in your day triggers an alert, you will stop reading them. Keep reminders for the things that are easy to miss: medicine, a call, the start of a study block, or a class. Routine items you always do can stay without a reminder.

2. Choose a lead time

Pick how long before a task you want the reminder. Five minutes is enough for a quick change of activity. Fifteen or thirty minutes suits things that need preparation, such as travelling to class. A lead time of zero means the reminder arrives exactly at the start time.

3. Allow notifications

Reminders are only useful if your device can show them. In your browser or phone settings, allow notifications for the planner. If notifications are blocked, the alert will not reach you even though the plan is correct.

4. Use sound and the tick to confirm

A short sound when a reminder appears helps you notice it while you are busy. The tick sound when you finish a task gives you a clear signal, and it can help a routine feel complete. Choose sounds you do not find annoying, because you will hear them many times a day.

5. Check reminders when you change the plan

After you move or edit a task, check that its reminder moved too. A reminder for an old time is worse than none, because it trains you to ignore alerts.

6. Keep a routine, not a rulebook

Reminders should support a routine you want, not punish you for missing one. If a reminder keeps arriving for something you have stopped doing, remove it or move it. Reviewing your reminders once a week keeps them useful.

In Daybreak: open the Function tab to turn reminders on or off and choose the lead time. Reminders appear as alerts in the app and, if you allow notifications, as system notifications while the planner is open.
