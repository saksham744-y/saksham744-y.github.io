How to plan revision for exams

Exam revision goes better with a plan that starts early and includes practice, not only reading. This guide shows how to build one from the exam dates backwards.

1. Count the days you have

Write the date of each exam and count the study days left before it. Keep one or two days free before the first exam as a buffer, because plans always slip a little. Your real number of study days is the count minus those buffer days.

2. List the topics and rank them

Write every topic on the syllabus. For each one, mark how confident you feel: strong, unsure or weak. Also mark how much it counts in the exam, if you know. Spend more time on weak topics that count for a lot, and less on strong topics you already know well.

3. Share the days out

Give each topic a number of sessions that matches its rank. A weak topic might need three or four sessions, and a strong one only a short review. Spread topics across the days rather than doing one subject for a week, so you come back to each one often.

4. Use short, focused sessions

A session of 45 to 60 minutes with a clear goal works better than a long session with no target. A goal such as “work through the past paper questions on forces” tells you when you are done.

5. Practise under exam conditions

Reading notes is not enough. Schedule past papers or practice questions in the last third of your plan, with a timer and no notes. Mark them and write down the mistakes, then revise those exact topics.

6. Rest on purpose

Sleep and breaks help memory. Keep a fixed bedtime during the exam period, and schedule at least one free half-day each week. A plan that exhausts you will fail on exam day.

7. Review the plan every few days

Once every few days, check what you finished and move unfinished sessions into the free days you kept. Do not try to make up a whole missed week in one night.
