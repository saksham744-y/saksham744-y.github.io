How to build a weekly timetable you will follow

A weekly timetable is easier to keep when it matches how your week really works. Plan the repeating parts first, then add the exceptions.

1. Separate the routine from the exceptions

Most weeks have a routine: the same classes, the same work hours, the same meals. Other things happen once, such as a dentist visit or a test on one Thursday. Keep these two kinds of items apart. Repeating items should be set once and shown on the days they happen. One-off items should be added only to the date they belong to.

2. Start with the days you already know

Take one ordinary weekday and write its schedule in time order. Then check whether Saturday and Sunday are different. Many people have a weekday timetable and a separate weekend timetable, and that is fine. Build each one separately rather than forcing both into one.

3. Leave room for travel and rest

Add time between items for getting from one place to another, and do not fill every hour. A timetable with no slack breaks at the first delay. A little free time in the middle of the day gives you room to catch up.

4. Review the week every Sunday

Spend ten minutes at the end of each week looking at the next one. Check the exams, deadlines and events you already know about, and move any routine items that clash with them. A short weekly review keeps the timetable close to reality.

5. Change the week without starting over

When one day is different, change that day only. Replacing the whole timetable for one unusual day makes it harder to see the routine that still applies. Keep the routine as it is, and add or change the single day.

6. Keep the timetable where you look every day

A timetable is useful only if you look at it. Pick one place to check it each morning, such as the first screen you open on your phone or computer, and read the day ahead before you start.
